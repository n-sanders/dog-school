## Why features matter

When experts identify a dog, they do not just look at the whole dog and guess.
They check a few **features** one at a time, then put the clues together. Learning
to do this makes you much faster and more accurate.

## The five features to check first

1. **Size** - Is the dog tiny enough to carry, or as tall as a table? Size groups
   go roughly from *toy* and *small* through *medium* and *large* to *giant*.
2. **Coat** - Is the hair short and smooth, long and silky, curly, or wiry
   (rough and bristly)? What colors and patterns does it have?
3. **Ears** - *Erect* ears stand straight up. *Drop* ears hang down. Some ears are
   in between: semi-erect ears stand up with the tips folded forward.
4. **Muzzle** - The muzzle is the nose and jaw. Some breeds have a long, narrow
   muzzle; others have a short, flat face.
5. **Tail** - Is it long or short? Carried low, straight up, or curled over the back?

## Watch out

- **Puppies** often look different from adults. Their ears and coats may not have
  grown in yet.
- **Haircuts** can hide a breed's natural coat.
- **Lighting** in a photo can make colors look darker or lighter than they really are.

Look at the example photos below and find each feature in the captions before
you take the quiz.

</details>

## Dogs were bred for jobs

Most breeds were developed to do a particular job: hunting, herding, guarding,
pulling sleds or keeping people company. Breeds that did similar jobs often look
alike, so knowing a dog's group is a great first clue.

## The seven AKC groups

The American Kennel Club (AKC) sorts recognized breeds into seven groups:

| Group | Original job | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sporting** | Helping hunters find and fetch birds | Golden Retriever |
| **Hound** | Hunting by sight or by scent | Beagle |
| **Working** | Guarding, pulling and rescue | Great Dane |
| **Terrier** | Hunting rats, foxes and other vermin | Airedale Terrier |
| **Toy** | Being a small companion | Chihuahua |
| **Non-sporting** | A mixed group of breeds that do not fit elsewhere | Poodle |
| **Herding** | Moving sheep and cattle | German Shepherd |

Other kennel clubs around the world use slightly different groups, but the idea
is the same.

## Using groups to identify dogs

When you see an unfamiliar dog, ask: *What job does this dog look built for?*
A long-legged, lean dog may be a sighthound. A small, wiry, bold dog may be a
terrier. A dog with a thick coat and a strong body may be a working or herding dog.

## Two famous retrievers

Labrador Retrievers and Golden Retrievers are both **sporting dogs** bred to
fetch birds for hunters. Both are large, friendly and very popular as family
dogs, so you will see them often.

## The Labrador Retriever

- **Coat:** short, dense and flat, like a seal's.
- **Color:** one solid color - black, yellow or chocolate.
- **Tail:** a thick "otter tail" that is wide at the base.

## The Golden Retriever

- **Coat:** medium-length and may be wavy.
- **Color:** always a shade of gold, from cream to deep red-gold.
- **Feathering:** long hair on the backs of the legs, the chest and the tail.

## The key clue

A yellow Labrador and a light Golden can be a similar color. **Do not rely on
color.** Look at the coat length and the tail: a short coat with no feathering
means Labrador; long, flowing feathers mean Golden.

## The Beagle

Beagles are small **hounds** bred to hunt rabbits in packs by following scent.

- **Size:** small and compact.
- **Coat:** short and hard, usually **tricolor** - a black "saddle" on the back,
  with tan and white.
- **Ears:** long, wide drop ears.
- **Tail:** carried up, often with a **white tip** so hunters could spot the dog
  in tall grass.

## The Standard Poodle

The Standard is the largest of the three poodle sizes (Standard, Miniature
and Toy). Poodles were originally water retrievers.

- **Coat:** dense and **curly**. It keeps growing, so poodles are often clipped
  into special styles.
- **Head:** a long, straight, fine muzzle and long, flat ears.
- **Body:** square and proud, with the head held high.
- **Color:** one solid color, such as white, black, apricot or gray.

## Tip

A curly coat is one of the easiest clues in dog identification. If you see tight
curls all over a large, elegant dog, think Poodle first.

## Two bulldogs

Both bulldogs have short, pushed-in faces, but they are easy to tell apart once
you know where to look.

## The Bulldog (English Bulldog)

- **Head:** massive, with heavy **wrinkles** and hanging upper lips ("chops").
- **Jaw:** undershot - the lower jaw sticks out past the upper jaw.
- **Body:** low, wide and heavy, with broad shoulders.
- **Ears:** small and thin, folding back ("rose ears").

## The French Bulldog

- **Ears:** large, upright **bat ears** - wide at the base and rounded on top.
- **Head:** square and flat on top, with a short muzzle.
- **Body:** small, compact and muscular - much lighter than the English Bulldog.

## The key clue

**Look at the ears.** Big upright ears mean French Bulldog. Small folded ears
on a heavy, wrinkled dog mean English Bulldog.

## Working partners

The German Shepherd Dog and the Belgian Malinois are both **herding** breeds
now widely used by police and the military. They have similar pointed ears and
a similar shape of head, so people mix them up.

## German Shepherd Dog

- **Build:** longer than tall, with a back that **slopes** toward the hindquarters.
- **Coat:** medium-length and thick.
- **Color:** most often black and tan or black and red, with a dark **saddle**
  across the back.
- **Tail:** bushy, hanging in a gentle curve.

## Belgian Malinois

- **Build:** square, lighter-boned and leaner, with a **level** back.
- **Coat:** **short** and straight.
- **Color:** fawn to mahogany, with black tips on the hairs, plus a black
  **mask** and black ears.

## The key clues

1. Short fawn coat and a black mask: **Malinois**.
2. Thicker coat with a black saddle and a sloping back: **German Shepherd**.

## Collie-type dogs

Collies come from Scotland and northern England, where they herded sheep. They
are clever, quick and often have long, flowing coats.

## Border Collie

- **Color:** usually **black and white**, with white on the face, neck, legs and
  tail tip.
- **Coat:** smooth (short) or rough (medium and feathered).
- **Build:** athletic and slightly longer than tall, with a long, low tail.
- **Look:** an intense, focused stare - the famous "herding eye".

## Rough Collie

- **Size:** large - about 22 to 26 inches at the shoulder.
- **Coat:** very long, with a big **mane** and frill around the neck.
- **Head:** long and wedge-shaped, with semi-erect ears that tip forward.

## Shetland Sheepdog (Sheltie)

- **Size:** small - about 13 to 16 inches at the shoulder.
- **Coat and head:** almost the same as the Rough Collie's - long coat, full mane,
  wedge-shaped head with tipped ears.

## The key clue

The Rough Collie and the Sheltie look like big and small versions of each other.
**Size is the main clue.** In a photo, look for something to compare against,
like a person, a chair or a doorway.

## The corgis

Both corgis come from Wales and were bred to herd cattle by nipping at their
heels. Their very short legs let them duck under kicks.

| Feature | Pembroke Welsh Corgi | Cardigan Welsh Corgi |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tail** | Short or docked | Long, fox-like brush tail |
| **Ears** | Erect, slightly pointed | Large, rounded, erect |
| **Colors** | Red, sable, fawn, black and tan | Wider range, including blue merle and brindle |
| **Build** | Long and low | Long and low, heavier-boned |

**The key clue is the tail.** No tail (or a tiny one): Pembroke. Long, bushy tail:

## Husky or Malamute?

| Clue | Siberian Husky | Alaskan Malamute |
| --- | --- | --- |

## Color is a trap

Yellow Labradors and light Golden Retrievers can be exactly the same color, so

## Whippet or Italian Greyhound?
