## Dogs were bred for jobs

Most breeds were developed to do a particular job: hunting, herding, guarding,
pulling sleds or keeping people company. Breeds that did similar jobs often look
alike, so knowing a dog's group is a great first clue.

## The seven AKC groups

The American Kennel Club (AKC) sorts recognized breeds into seven groups:

| Group | Original job | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sporting** | Helping hunters find and fetch birds | Golden Retriever |
| **Hound** | Hunting by sight or by scent | Beagle |
| **Working** | Guarding, pulling and rescue | Great Dane |
| **Terrier** | Hunting rats, foxes and other vermin | Airedale Terrier |
| **Toy** | Being a small companion | Chihuahua |
| **Non-sporting** | A mixed group of breeds that do not fit elsewhere | Poodle |
| **Herding** | Moving sheep and cattle | German Shepherd |

Other kennel clubs around the world use slightly different groups, but the idea
is the same.

## Using groups to identify dogs

When you see an unfamiliar dog, ask: *What job does this dog look built for?*
A long-legged, lean dog may be a sighthound. A small, wiry, bold dog may be a
terrier. A dog with a thick coat and a strong body may be a working or herding dog.
